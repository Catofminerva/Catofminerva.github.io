So this post is going to be different than the usual and is more like a personal log of today's happening, only because I need to share what happened somewhere and I don't have social media. My life is pretty unremarkable so this event was a remark-able thing for me, but I appreicate may be mundane to readers.

Last night the maid, Swaida, found a baby kitten outside. Couple days old, umbilical cord still attached to it, etc. I did not find out about any of this until  the following afternoon, which is roughly eighteen hours of a neonate existing in my house without my knowledge, and by the time I was told the situation had already curdled into an emergency that I was somehow expected to solve immediately.

I went and bought kitten formula but it thoroghly rejected it. It also would not poop on its own because of course it wouldn't, being as it is, a tiny baby. Meanwhile the kittens I adopted a few months ago ('adopted' may give the impression that I did so enthusiasitcaly but I did so in the sense that the alternative was watching them die, which is basically how every single one of the dozens of cats currently eating my salary has entered my life) had gotten into the formula and distributed the powder across the entire floor and then knocked over and shattered the glass of warm water I had frantically prepared to mix it in, all of this while the baby was screaming in the background.

I need people to understand what kitten crying does to me. It is not a normal sound to me. It goes past the part of my human brain that makes decisions and lands somewhere in my monkey brain that is much older and much stupider and my entire body responds as though I am personally a mother cat who has failed at her one job. So my hands covered in milk and poop, powder everywhere, broken glass (which the kittens loved stepping on for some reason), humid and 38 degrees celsius outside, and a tiny little creature not even the size of my palms incessantly making the one noise on earth that I cannot tolerate. I was, in short, having a full mental breakdown on the floor of my own house.

Swaida to her credit had been out looking for the mother all over the neighbourhood since the night before. Then the driver Shafeeg decided to climb onto the roof of the outdoor kitchen. The roof connects to the building we use for the pool, which is connected to the majles, and which in turn connects to the neighbours' house, so he went across the whole stretch of rooftops jumping from one to the next, and eventually shouted down that he had found something that looked like a mother cat.

I  honestly assumed it was over and that probably it was just a random cat or that even if by some miracle it was the right one, the kitten had been handled by humans for a full day now and the queen would surely want nothing to do with it. But what the heck, I thought, let's give it a go. Shafeeg could not carry the kitten across himself since the walls on that side are too tall, and he would have had to make the roof jumps one-handed with a two-day-old animal. So I put the kitten in a box, knotted rope around the box and walked around to the opposite side of the wall from where he had climbed up. I threw him the rope, fastened the box to the end and he hauled it up and set it down maybe two feet away from the cat while the kitten was still crying.

Within seconds! She picked it up by the scruff, turned around, and jumped off the roof with it, presumably heading back to wherever the rest of the litter is.

I could not believe it. I have never been this happy about anything!
